Issue No. 04 · Usually 6–10

So you want a sleepover

The question is almost never whether your child is ready. It is whether you know the house - and whether your kid has a working exit at one in the morning that does not cost them any dignity.

What you are actually deciding

Start here, because the wrong question wastes the conversation

The real question

Not are they old enough. It is: do I know this home, and does my child have a way out they will actually use?

  • Whose house, and which adults are there overnight
  • Who else is staying
  • What the pickup plan is, exactly

Facts worth knowing

The failure mode is not drama. It is a quiet child at 1am who has decided that asking to go home would be embarrassing, and who then says nothing for five hours.

A child will use an escape hatch they are certain costs them nothing. They will not use one they have to argue for at midnight, in someone else’s kitchen, in front of a friend.

Ask all six. Tick as you go. Most parents ask these and nobody minds being asked.

Say this, not that

Adapt the words, keep the structure

Say

Two things and then yes. I am going to ring their mum and ask a few boring questions. And you can come home at any hour, no embarrassment, no questions until the morning. That is not failing. That is the deal.

Puts the exit in place before it is needed, and frames the call as routine rather than as suspicion of that family.

On the doorstep, quietly

Text me the word and I will just come. You do not have to explain anything to anyone.

The code word exists so they never have to say the sentence out loud in front of their friend.

Not this

You’ll be fine!

It sounds kind and it quietly forecloses the one sentence you most need them to be able to say, which is I am not fine.

A code word, and a no-questions pickup. One text, you drive, nobody discusses it until morning.

Make the exit free and they will use it early. Make it cost something and they will endure the whole night instead.

The longer version

If you have ten minutes rather than two

The boring questions, and how to ask them

Ring rather than text, keep it light, and ask them the way you would ask about a swimming pool - because that is exactly what they are: ordinary safety questions that most parents ask each other and nobody minds.

If any answer is evasive, that is information. You are allowed to say ‘I think we will do a late-over this time’ without giving a reason.

If it goes wrong at 1am

Go. Do not negotiate on the phone, do not ask what happened, do not suggest they try to sleep. Drive.

In the morning, ask once, gently, and accept a short answer. A child who is collected without fuss will do a sleepover again in a month. A child who is talked out of leaving may not do one for a year.

If it is not yet

A late-over. All of the party, collected at nine, and nobody has to be brave about it. Most children who are not ready for the night are entirely ready for the evening.
