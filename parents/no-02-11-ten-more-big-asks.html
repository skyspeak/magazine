big asks

Issue No. 01 took one decision apart at full length. These are the ten that follow it — each its own issue, same shape every time, so you can find the thing you need the night before you need it.

10
No. 02 So you want a phoneUsually 10–13 Are they ready to be reachable by anybody, at any hour, with no adult in the room? No. 03 So you want to be onlineUsually 12–15 Who can see them, who can message them, and what happens to it afterwards? No. 04 So you want a sleepoverUsually 6–10 Do I know this home, and does my child have a way out they will actually use? No. 05 So you want to go on your ownUsually 8–12 Does my kid have the judgement for this specific journey, and do they know what to do when it goes wrong? No. 06 So you want to quitAny age, repeatedly Which part do they hate - the thing, the person, the practising, or the level? Four different problems, four different fixes. No. 07 So you want to stay home aloneUsually 9–13 Do they know what to do about the doorbell, the alarm, the locked door, and not being able to reach me? No. 08 So you want your own moneyUsually 7–14 Am I actually going to let them lose it? No. 09 So you want a jobUsually 13–17 Whose time is it now, and what happens the first time work and school collide? No. 10 So you want to change how you lookUsually 10–16 Is this reversible - and if it is, why am I in the conversation at all? No. 11 So you want to go out with someoneUsually 13–17 Am I someone they can report to without a scene?

Not professional advice. These are conversation structures, not rules. Nothing here is medical, legal, psychological or safeguarding guidance. Age bands are rough and your child is not a band. Anything touching a child's safety belongs with people who know your family.
