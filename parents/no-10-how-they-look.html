Issue No. 10 · Usually 10–16

So you want to change how you look

Sort every request onto one axis and most of the argument disappears: grows back, washes out, or does not. Hair grows. Dye fades. Most holes close. Tattoos do not.

What you are actually deciding

Start here, because the wrong question wastes the conversation

The real question

Not do I like it. It is: is this reversible - and if it is, why am I in the conversation at all?

  • Who pays

Facts worth knowing

Almost every fight in this category is about a reversible thing. Parents spend their authority on hair colour and have none left for the permanent list.

You get a limited number of vetoes per teenager. Green hair is a poor place to spend one, and they will remember exactly which hill you chose.

Sort the list before the argumentNothing here is saved or sent

Put each one where it belongs. The point is how few land in the last column.

Say this, not that

Adapt the words, keep the structure

Say

My rule is simple. If it grows back or washes out, it is your call. If it does not, we wait until you are sixteen - and then it is still your call.

A rule they can predict, apply themselves, and argue with on its merits rather than on your mood.

When you hate it

Not what I would have picked. Your head though.

Say it warmly and then genuinely drop it. Withholding approval while granting permission is worse than refusing.

Not this

What will your grandmother say?

It moves the decision from their body to other people’s opinions, and turns a small aesthetic choice into a fight about control - which you will lose eventually anyway, on a worse day.

Reversible is theirs. Save your no for the permanent list.

It is a rule that is legible from the outside, which means it does not feel like arbitrary power - and that is what makes it survivable.

The longer version

If you have ten minutes rather than two

The sorting rule, in practice

Hair colour, length, style, makeup, nail varnish, most clothing: reversible. Default yes. The cost of a bad haircut is six weeks of a bad haircut, which is a lesson delivered free.

Ear piercing: mostly reversible, closes over, common from young. Cartilage and body piercings are slower to heal and more prone to infection - a real reason for a real wait, and one that is worth explaining as such.

Tattoos: not reversible, and almost everywhere illegal below eighteen anyway, which usefully makes it not your rule.

What this is usually actually about

At this age appearance is how identity gets tested in public. The request is rarely about the hair; it is about being the author of something visible.

Which is why granting the reversible ones cheaply is such a good trade. They get authorship, you keep your vetoes, and the whole category stops being a battleground - so that when something genuinely permanent comes up, you are a person whose no means something.

If it is not yet

For permanent things: a date, not a lecture. And say plainly that the answer at that date is very likely yes - a ‘not yet’ that is secretly a no is the thing that ends honest conversation.
