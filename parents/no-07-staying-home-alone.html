Issue No. 07 · Usually 9–13

So you want to stay home alone

You are not really assessing whether they can be trusted alone. You are assessing what they do when something goes wrong - and the things that go wrong are far more boring than the ones you are picturing.

What you are actually deciding

Start here, because the wrong question wastes the conversation

The real question

Not are they mature enough. It is: do they know what to do about the doorbell, the alarm, the locked door, and not being able to reach me?

  • How long, and how often
  • Whether a sibling is company or a second job
  • What is off limits - oven, bath, front door

Facts worth knowing

First-time-alone problems are almost always mundane: a delivery, a locked door, burnt toast setting off the alarm, a sibling fight. Rehearse those four and you have covered most of it.

Leaving a much younger sibling in their care is a different decision from leaving them alone, and it is usually a later one. Being responsible for yourself and being responsible for a five-year-old are not the same job.

Each duration needs a different amount of preparation.

Say this, not that

Adapt the words, keep the structure

Say

Twenty minutes while I get milk. Before I go: tell me what you do if someone knocks, if you get locked out, and if you ring me and I do not pick up.

Short, specific, and rehearsed out loud rather than assumed.

When you get back

How was it? Anything happen? Right - next time I will be gone about an hour.

Names the ladder. They should know the next rung exists.

Not this

Just don’t open the door to anyone.

One prohibition and no plan. They need a script for the four boring emergencies, not a single rule that leaves them frozen when the smoke alarm goes off.

Start at twenty minutes, and come back exactly when you said you would.

The first reliability being tested here is yours. A parent who is an hour late teaches the house that stated times are approximate - and that is the lesson that comes back at sixteen.

The longer version

If you have ten minutes rather than two

Rehearse the four boring emergencies

The door. Agreed answer: do not open it. Deliveries can be left. If someone will not go away, ring me.

The alarm. If it is toast, open a window. If there is any actual smoke, out of the house first and ring from outside - do not investigate.

Locked out. A named neighbour, or a spot to wait that is not the doorstep. Decide it now.

Cannot reach you. A second adult by name, and the reassurance that this is normal - phones die and signal drops, and it does not mean anything has happened to you.

The sibling question

Two children alone is not half the risk, it is a different one: the risk is now a fight rather than an accident. Agree in advance who is in charge of what, and be explicit that being older does not mean being the boss of everything.

If one child is much younger, that is babysitting, and it deserves its own conversation, its own rules, and - once they are old enough for it to be a real job - probably its own pay.

If it is not yet

Alone in the house while you are in the garden. Then while you walk to the end of the street. The ladder here is measured in minutes, and it climbs quickly.
