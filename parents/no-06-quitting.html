Issue No. 06 · Any age, repeatedly

So you want to quit

The useful question is not whether to let them quit. It is whether they are quitting the activity or quitting a bad week - and those need opposite answers.

What you are actually deciding

Start here, because the wrong question wastes the conversation

The real question

Not do we allow quitting. It is: which part do they hate - the thing, the person, the practising, or the level? Four different problems, four different fixes.

  • Quit, change teacher or team, or change the amount
  • When the exit happens
  • What they owe the other people involved

Facts worth knowing

The urge to quit usually peaks just before competence, not at the start. The plateau - where effort stops producing visible improvement - is the most common place a child asks to stop.

‘We don’t quit things’ teaches that stopping anything is a defect of character. It is the belief that keeps a twenty-six-year-old in a job they hate.

Which part do they actually hate?Nothing here is saved or sent

Ask them, then pick. The four have genuinely different answers.

Say this, not that

Adapt the words, keep the structure

Say

You can quit. Not today - at the end of term, so you are leaving from a decision instead of a bad Tuesday. And I want to know which part you hate, because if it is the teacher we have a much easier problem.

Grants the thing, sets a boundary, and starts the diagnosis in one move.

Three weeks later

Still want to stop? Then we stop, and I am not going to make you feel bad about it.

Keeping this promise is the whole exercise. If you renege here, they will not tell you the truth next time.

Not this

We don’t quit things in this family.

It does not produce persistence. It produces children who conceal that they are miserable, because misery has been defined as a character flaw.

Quit at a boundary, not in a bad moment - and finish what other people are counting on.

The lesson worth keeping is not persistence for its own sake. It is that you exit cleanly and you do not leave a team short.

The longer version

If you have ten minutes rather than two

Diagnose before you decide

Children almost never say ‘the teacher humiliates me in front of the group’. They say ‘I hate piano’. The sentence you get is a summary, and the summary is usually wrong.

So ask about the parts. When in the week do you dread it? What is the worst bit - going, being there, or the practising at home? Is there anyone there you like? The answers sort the problem quickly.

Quitting well is a skill worth teaching

Tell the teacher or coach themselves, in person or on the phone. Thank them. Finish the commitment. Return the kit.

A child who has quit one thing well is far better equipped than one who has never been allowed to stop anything - because at some point they will need to leave a job, a course, or a relationship, and this is the rehearsal.

How you will know

And what to do if the answer is not yet

If it is not yet

Three more weeks with exactly one thing changed, and a promise you will honour whatever they say at the end of it. Then honour it - that is the part that counts.
