Issue No. 09 · Usually 13–17

So you want a job

The money is the least interesting part. The real curriculum is having a boss who is not a parent - someone who corrects them, and whom they have to say no to.

What you are actually deciding

Start here, because the wrong question wastes the conversation

The real question

Not do they need the money. It is: whose time is it now, and what happens the first time work and school collide?

  • An hours cap, agreed in writing
  • The school-night rule
  • What happens to the money

Facts worth knowing

School performance generally holds up to somewhere around ten to fifteen hours a week and starts to slide beyond it. Agree the cap before the first shift, not after the first bad report.

The most valuable hour of a first job is the one where they have to tell a manager they cannot work Saturday. Do not take that hour away from them by making the call yourself.

Say this, not that

Adapt the words, keep the structure

Say

Yes. The cap is twelve hours, nothing after nine on a school night, and we look at it again in a month. What you earn is yours - I would like some of it to go somewhere it can sit.

Specific, time-limited, and it hands over the money properly rather than conditionally.

When the rota clashes with an exam

That is yours to sort out. What are you going to say to them?

Rehearse it with them if they want. Do not make the call.

Not this

I’ll ring your manager and sort the rota.

You have just removed the only part of the job that was going to teach them anything they could not learn from pocket money.

They make the phone calls. Including the awkward one where they cannot work Saturday.

Asking an unrelated adult for something, out loud, is the skill. Everything else is logistics.

The longer version

If you have ten minutes rather than two

What the money is for

Resist assigning it. An allowance you control teaches budgeting; a wage you control teaches nothing, because it is not really theirs.

One reasonable ask: some proportion goes somewhere it cannot be spent this week. Frame it as a suggestion with a reason, not a condition of the job, and let them choose the number.

The first bad week

There will be a week where they are tired, behind, and regretting it. That is the week the whole thing is worth - and the temptation to say I told you so will be enormous.

Do not. Ask what they want to change: fewer hours, different shifts, or out. Then let them execute it themselves. A teenager who negotiates their own hours down has learned something no lecture delivers.

If it is not yet

One-off paid work first - babysitting, a neighbour’s garden, a weekend of something. Same lesson, no rota, and it tells you a great deal about how they will handle the real thing.
