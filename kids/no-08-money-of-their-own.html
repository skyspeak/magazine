So you want your own money

What they’re actually scared of, what to say, and the one job that does the work.

The short version

They’re scared ofPaying for the same thing twice.
SayIf I buy something rubbish, don’t replace it.
Don’t say“Can I have it early?” Not once. Ever.
Your jobSave for one thing all the way to the end, where they can watch you do it.
If they say not yetAsk for less, weekly, in cash.

Why that works

Saying ‘don’t replace it if I waste it’ sounds mad. It is actually the single most convincing sentence available to you, because it is exactly what they’re worried about.

And asking for an advance is the one thing that ends this fastest. Once you’ve borrowed against next week, you are not someone with their own money - you’re someone with a debt.

What to actually do

  1. Ask for a small amount, weekly, in cash. Small gets a yes much faster than big.
  2. Say up front that you don’t want rescuing if you waste it. Then don’t ask to be rescued.
  3. Save for one thing, right to the end, without giving up halfway. One completed save changes what they think of you.

Ten weeks of saving something

Any amount. The point is not the total - it’s that you didn’t stop.

For the grown-up reading over a shoulder

This is the child’s half of Issue No. 08. Nothing here contradicts yours. It tells them that asking repeatedly fails, that you are worried about something specific and findable, and that “not yet” is a real answer if it comes with a date. If you are going to say not yet, please bring the date.
