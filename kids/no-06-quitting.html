So you want to quit

What they’re actually scared of, what to say, and the one job that does the work.

The short version

They’re scared ofThat you’ll quit everything from now on.
SayI want to stop at the end of term. Not today.
Don’t say“I hate it.” That’s a noise, not information.
Your jobWork out which bit you hate: the thing, the person, the practising, or that it got hard.
If they say not yetAsk to change one thing and try three more weeks.

Why that works

Saying ‘I want to quit’ on a bad day almost never works, because they can tell it’s the day talking. Saying it calmly, twice, three weeks apart, works much better.

And here’s the useful bit: if what you actually hate is the coach, or the practising, that’s a much smaller problem and you can probably keep the good parts.

What to actually do

  1. Work out which of the four bits you hate. Be honest with yourself first.
  2. Ask to stop at the end of term, not today. It makes you sound like someone deciding, not sulking.
  3. Offer to try one change first - different class, less practice. It usually gets you a yes either way.

Which bit do you actually hate?

Be honest. It changes what you should ask for.

For the grown-up reading over a shoulder

This is the child’s half of Issue No. 06. Nothing here contradicts yours. It tells them that asking repeatedly fails, that you are worried about something specific and findable, and that “not yet” is a real answer if it comes with a date. If you are going to say not yet, please bring the date.
