02

A phone

They’re scared ofYou disappearing into it.
SayCan we start with one that just calls and texts?
Don’t say“Everyone has one.” They know. It has never once worked.
Your jobPut it in the kitchen at night before they ask you to.
If they say not yetAsk what you’d have to show them. Then show them, for a month.
03

Being online

They’re scared ofStrangers, and stuff that never goes away.
SayYou can follow my account. I’d rather you saw it than found it.
Don’t say“You don’t get it.” Maybe not. Doesn’t help you.
Your jobTell them one thing that happened online before they hear it from someone else.
If they say not yetAsk for a group chat with people you both know.
04

A sleepover

They’re scared ofYou at 1am, in a house they’ve never been in.
SayIf I want to come home I’ll text you. I won’t be embarrassed.
Don’t say“I’ll be fine!” Then at 1am you’re stuck being fine.
Your jobPick a code word together. Boring. Works.
If they say not yetAsk for a late‑over. All the fun, home at nine.
05

Going on your own

They’re scared ofRoads. Honestly, mostly roads.
SayCan we walk it together three times first?
Don’t say“Nothing’s going to happen.” You can’t promise that.
Your jobSay out loud what you’d do if the shop’s shut and your phone’s dead. Before they ask.
If they say not yetAsk for half the route.
06

Quitting

They’re scared ofThat you’ll quit everything from now on.
SayI want to stop at the end of term. Not today.
Don’t say“I hate it.” That’s a noise, not information.
Your jobWork out which bit you hate: the thing, the person, or the practising. Three different problems.
If they say not yetAsk to change one thing and try three more weeks.
07

Staying home alone

They’re scared ofThe doorbell. And you not picking up.
SayGive me twenty minutes while you get milk.
Don’t say“I’m not a baby.” True. Still annoying.
Your jobAnswer your phone. Every time. Miss it once and this gets very slow.
If they say not yetAsk for ten minutes while they’re in the garden.
08

Your own money

They’re scared ofPaying for the same thing twice.
SayIf I buy something rubbish, don’t replace it.
Don’t say“Can I have it early?” Not once. Ever.
Your jobSave for one thing all the way to the end, where they can watch you do it.
If they say not yetAsk for less, weekly, in cash.
09

A job

They’re scared ofYour sleep.
SayCap my hours, and check my grades in a month.
Don’t say“It won’t affect anything.” It will. Say so first.
Your jobMake the phone calls yourself. Including the awkward one.
If they say not yetAsk to do one paid thing first. Babysitting. Someone’s garden.
10

How you look

They’re scared ofOnly the permanent ones. That’s genuinely it.
SayThis one washes out. Can that one be mine?
Don’t say“It’s my body.” True. Still loses the argument.
Your jobSplit your list into grows‑back and doesn’t. Ask for the grows‑back ones first.
If they say not yetGet a date for the permanent one. Then drop it until then.
11

Going out with someone

They’re scared ofYou telling them nothing at all.
SayDo you want to meet them?
Don’t saySaying nothing. Silence makes grown‑ups invent things.
Your jobTell them one boring true thing early. It buys you privacy about the rest.
If they say not yetAsk exactly what’s allowed right now. Exactly.

For the grown‑up reading over a shoulder

This is the child’s edition of Ten More Big Asks. Nothing here contradicts yours. It tells them three things: that asking repeatedly fails, that you are worried about something specific and findable, and that “not yet” is a real answer if it comes with a date. If you are going to say not yet, please bring the date.
