So you want to be online

What they’re actually scared of, what to say, and the one job that does the work.

The short version

They’re scared ofStrangers, and stuff that never goes away.
SayYou can follow my account. I’d rather you saw it than found it.
Don’t say“You don’t get it.” Maybe not. Doesn’t help you.
Your jobTell them one thing that happened online before they hear it from someone else.
If they say not yetAsk for a group chat with people you both know.

Why that works

Offering to be followed sounds like giving something up. It isn’t. It’s the fastest way to get an account at all, and after about two weeks they stop looking.

The other one is harder and worth more. If something weird happens online and you tell them first, you become a person who reports things. That’s worth a lot of freedom later.

What to actually do

  1. Set it private together, on the first day. It’s much easier than going private later.
  2. Ask them straight out: what would make you take this away? Then you know where the edges are.
  3. Tell them one small thing that happened online this week. Boring is fine. Doing it is the point.

Before you ask, can you answer these?

If you can answer all five, you’re making a much stronger case than most people your age.

For the grown-up reading over a shoulder

This is the child’s half of Issue No. 03. Nothing here contradicts yours. It tells them that asking repeatedly fails, that you are worried about something specific and findable, and that “not yet” is a real answer if it comes with a date. If you are going to say not yet, please bring the date.
