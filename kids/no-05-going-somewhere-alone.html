So you want to go on your own

What they’re actually scared of, what to say, and the one job that does the work.

The short version

They’re scared ofRoads. Honestly, mostly roads.
SayCan we walk it together three times first?
Don’t say“Nothing’s going to happen.” You can’t promise that, so don’t.
Your jobSay out loud what you’d do if the shop’s shut and your phone’s dead. Before they ask.
If they say not yetAsk for half the route.

Why that works

Grown-ups look worried about this one because of films. Actually the thing they’re right to worry about is traffic, which is boring and fixable.

So show them you’re good at roads, not that you’re brave. Being brave is not the thing being tested here.

What to actually do

  1. Ask to walk it together three times, and you do the deciding at the crossings.
  2. Learn one phone number off by heart. Not in your phone - in your head.
  3. Tell them your plan for three things going wrong. Shop shut. Friend not there. Phone dead.

For the grown-up reading over a shoulder

This is the child’s half of Issue No. 05. Nothing here contradicts yours. It tells them that asking repeatedly fails, that you are worried about something specific and findable, and that “not yet” is a real answer if it comes with a date. If you are going to say not yet, please bring the date.
