So you want a phone

What they’re actually scared of, what to say, and the one job that does the work.

The short version

They’re scared ofYou disappearing into it.
SayCan we start with one that just calls and texts?
Don’t say“Everyone has one.” They know. It has never once worked.
Your jobPut it in the kitchen at night before they ask you to.
If they say not yetAsk what you’d have to show them. Then show them, for a month.

Why that works

Here’s the thing your grown-up is actually picturing: you, at midnight, awake, on a screen. Not you being irresponsible. Just you not sleeping.

So the fastest way to a yes is to take that picture away before they mention it. If the phone lives in the kitchen at night, most of what they’re worried about is already solved, and you didn’t even have to argue.

What to actually do

  1. Ask for the small version first - calls and texts. It is much easier to say yes to.
  2. Say where it will sleep, before they ask. Kitchen. Every night.
  3. Ask when you can talk about apps. Get an actual date and then don’t bring it up until then.

Seven nights

If you already have any device - a tablet, a console, an old phone - park it outside your room at night. Seven nights. Say nothing.

For the grown-up reading over a shoulder

This is the child’s half of Issue No. 02. Nothing here contradicts yours. It tells them that asking repeatedly fails, that you are worried about something specific and findable, and that “not yet” is a real answer if it comes with a date. If you are going to say not yet, please bring the date.
