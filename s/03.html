Issue No. 03 · Usually 12–15

So you want to be online

You are deciding whether your child gets a public: a room of people they cannot see, which keeps a copy of everything. Screen time is a different argument, and a much smaller one.

What you are deciding

Start here. The wrong question wastes the conversation

The question

Who can see them, who can message them, and what happens to all of it afterwards.

  • Public account or private
  • Who is allowed to send them a message
  • Whether you are in the audience

Facts worth knowing

Thirteen is the platforms’ own minimum, and it is a legal floor rather than a recommendation of readiness. Nobody at those companies has met your child.

Private-by-default costs nothing on day one and is a humiliation to impose in month six. This is the same shape as the phone-in-the-bedroom rule, and it is the same mistake.

What are you worried about?Nothing here is saved or sent

They need different answers. Picking the wrong one wastes the conversation.

Say this, not that

Adapt the words, keep the structure

Say

One condition. I get to see it. Not to police you, because you are thirteen, and I am not sending you into a room full of adults without knowing what the room is like.

Names the reason as protection rather than distrust, which is both kinder and true.

The sentence that keeps the channel open

If something happens on there that upsets you, you can tell me and I will not take the account away.

Most children do not report because they expect the thing they love to be confiscated. Remove that fear and you get told things.

Not this

Give me your phone. Now.

A surprise inspection buys one afternoon of information and costs every future disclosure. Visibility agreed in advance costs nothing and lasts.

The first account is private, and you are in the audience. Renegotiate on a named date, not on a good mood.

You are not trying to read their messages. You are trying to know the size and shape of the room.

The longer version

If you have ten minutes rather than two

The three things that actually go wrong

Contact from people who should not be in touch. Private accounts and closed DMs handle most of it.

Content they were not ready for. Filters reduce this; nothing eliminates it. Plan for the conversation afterwards rather than the wall beforehand.

Comparison, which is the quiet one, and the one no setting fixes. It shows up as mood rather than as an incident, and you will notice it before they name it.

What 'I get to see it' should mean

Not reading their messages. Following the account, knowing the handle, and being able to look at the public half without an argument.

Agree the terms out loud: what you will look at, what you will not, and what would make you look closer. A teenager can live with a boundary they helped draw. They cannot live with an unpredictable one.

How you will know

And what to do if the answer is not yet

Ready whenNothing here is saved or sent

Tick honestly. This is not a quiz you are meant to pass.

If it is not yet

A group chat with people you both know, first. Same skills, knowable room, and you find out how they behave in a group before the group is strangers.
