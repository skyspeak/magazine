Issue No. 02 · Usually 10–13

So you want a phone

A phone is not one decision, it is four: a device, a phone number, an open internet, and an app store. They arrive in the same box, so most families hand over all four at once. You can grant them separately, and separating them is the whole game.

What you are deciding

Start here. The wrong question wastes the conversation

The question

Whether they are ready to be reachable by anybody, at any hour, with no adult in the room.

  • Which of the four you are giving
  • Where it sleeps
  • Who is allowed to contact them

Facts worth knowing

Whatever rule you set on day one is simply how phones work in this house. The same rule introduced six months later is a punishment for something, and will be argued with as one.

The bedroom is the whole ballgame. A phone that charges in the kitchen removes most of what parents are afraid of, and it costs you one conversation instead of a hundred.

Pick the one you are ready for. Each has a different rule attached.

Say this, not that

Adapt the words, keep the structure

Say

You can have a phone. It is for calling and texting us and your friends. The internet and apps are a separate conversation, and we will have it in six months.

Two grants, not four. The other two are not refused, they are scheduled.

And when the six months come

Right, apps. Which three, and what does each one do? Pick three and we will start there.

Three chosen apps is a decision they participated in. An open store is not.

Not this

If your grades slip I am taking it away.

The moment the phone is the currency for everything else, every conversation in your house becomes a phone conversation. Homework, chores, moods, all of it. Keep the phone rules about the phone.

The phone sleeps outside the bedroom. Everyone’s does, including yours.

It is nearly frictionless on day one and nearly impossible to introduce later. It also happens to be the single rule that protects sleep, which is the thing that degrades first.

The longer version

If you have ten minutes rather than two

What each of the four costs you

The device is the least of it. A phone that only makes calls solves the entire problem you started with, which was usually 'I need to reach them after football'.

The number means anyone who has it can reach them at 11pm. Worth knowing who has it.

The open internet is the one people mean when they say 'a phone'. It is a browser, and a browser is everything.

The app store is a different thing again: it is the ability to install a new social world overnight, without telling you. This is the grant to give last and slowest.

The handover conversation, in four minutes

Do it once, calmly, with the phone on the table between you rather than in their hands. Say what it is for. Say where it sleeps. Say what you will do if something goes wrong, and make that sentence generous, because it is the sentence they will remember at the moment it matters.

Then hand it over and stop talking about it. A phone that gets a lecture every week becomes the thing they hide.
